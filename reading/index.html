What to Do Before Our Reading Crew Arrives

The hour between finding water and the crew arriving is when a homeowner in Reading can do the most good. Look after people first and property second. If water is near outlets, cords or the electrical panel, stay out of it, and switch off power to that area only if you can reach the breaker without stepping in water. Water that came up from a drain or sewer line should be left completely alone.

Then shut the water off. Use the small valve behind the toilet or under the sink, or go straight to the main. After that, carry light items such as rugs, documents, electronics and chairs somewhere dry. Put foil or plastic under the legs of heavy furniture so the wood does not stain the carpet.

Take photos and video of every affected room before you clean up, because your insurer will want to see the damage as it was. If the water is clean and shallow, blot what you can, and let our Reading crew handle everything else.

Water Damage We See Most in Reading

Ask anyone on our crew what fills the schedule in Reading and you will hear the same six answers. They are all common, and with every one of them waiting adds to the repair.

Mold: Most mold growth is the follow-up to an earlier leak that seemed to be taken care of. Surfaces dry with towels and a box fan, yet the pad, subfloor and inside of the wall hold moisture. Since colonies can form in 24 to 48 hours, the first hint is often an earthy smell or speckled drywall weeks later.

Hoses: Every sink, toilet and washer is fed by a flexible connector that holds full house pressure around the clock. A burst connector keeps spraying until somebody closes a valve, and that may take a long while in an empty house. The low-cost defense is to swap out aging hoses and learn where the main shutoff is.

Pumps: In homes that have one, a sump pump works unnoticed for years and then quits on the one night it is needed. What we usually see is a jammed float, a motor that has worn out or a breaker that tripped. That leaves the lowest level under a few inches of water, with flooring, wall bases and stored belongings soaked. A battery backup and a quick test before the rainy part of the year go a long way.

Toilets: Among bathroom emergencies in Reading, the toilet that will not stop rising tops the list. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. Either way it moves under tile edges and vanity cabinets, so the floor needs to be checked with a meter.

Cooling: Few people think about the small pipe that carries condensation away from an AC system. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. The leak is gradual, so drywall and insulation may be soaked long before you spot a mark. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. Our Allentown Restoration Co crews extract it, remove what cannot be cleaned and dry the structure to verified readings.
